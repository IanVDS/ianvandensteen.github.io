Mechanical design

Robotic Arm

A multi-joint arm designed in Onshape, with joint loading, motor sizing, and a rigid rotating base treated as first-class design constraints.

  • Onshape
  • BLDC motors
  • Bearings
  • Slewing ring
Image / GIF / video

Overview

This project focused on building a robotic arm where mechanical decisions — not just kinematics — drive the design. The goal was a structure that can take real torque at the joints without flexing the base or overloading bearings under expected payloads.

Design approach

The full assembly was modeled in Onshape, iterating on link geometry, joint stacks, and mounting interfaces before committing to hardware. Each joint was sized around expected loads rather than catalogue convenience.

  • BLDC motor selection — matched continuous and peak torque to joint demands, including gearing and duty cycle.
  • Bearing selection — chose arrangements that handle combined radial and axial loads at the joints without binding under misalignment.
  • Slewing ring base — a rotating base using a slewing ring for a stiff, low-friction yaw axis that keeps the rest of the arm stable under moment loads.

Highlights

  • End-to-end CAD assembly with manufacturable joint interfaces
  • Motor and bearing choices driven by load estimates, not guesswork
  • Slewing-ring base for a rigid, continuous-rotation foundation
